## At first...
I got this project as an assignment that I was given a week to complete. At first, I thought to myself "How on earth am I ever going to get this done?" I had never sewn before. I had never picked up a needle and threaded it. When I saw the project I was being asked to complete, I started asking myself if there were enough hours in the week for me to even finish this. I spent the rest of class quietly panicked, and decided I would get started that very night. That evening, as I got ready to put on some tv and call it a night, I fumbled with my embroidery hoop until the fabric was held somewhat tightly, and I began to look up how to back stitch. We had practiced running stitch in class (just pulling the needle up through the fabric, then pushing it back down a bit farther forward repeatedly) and I could do that, but I was still pretty intimidated by these confusing diagrams I kept seeing when I tried to learn another stitch. I found a video, mimicked it slowly, and started to back stitch. Little did I know, it didn't turn out to be that different. After stabbing myself a couple times with the needle, pulling thread from my knot through a stitch, and having to rethread my needle more than I could count, I looked up from my sampler, looked back down at it, and started to feel reassured. Here I was, with an episode of Silicon Valley's worth of effort, and I was halfway through the "K" in "Kenyon" at the top of the sampler. With this relief, I pushed on. I started the second half of the "K" with small, tight stitches I would need to make a nice swoop. By the time I was about ready to fall asleep, I was almost done with the "K", so I set my sampler down and turned off the lights. 
The next morning, as I got my backpack ready for class, I took my sampler with me. As I put it in my bag, I realized something - I had traced the "K" I had been working on on the wrong side. I had the stitched on the right side, but since my trace was upside down, it was backwards. I left my sampler at home and went to class.
Dejected, I cut a new sampler when I got home that night. I started over, tracing designs on the right side this time, and doing my best not to stab myself any more than I already had. It took about a half hour this time to make up what I had spent about 45 minutes on last night. I continued. By the end of the night, I had finished "Kenyon" in its entirety. 
## By this point,
I could start to see the light at the end of the tunnel. This work was time consuming, frustrating, and was easy to mess up, but it was also an exercise in learning to be consistent. Once I felt confident at  stitch, working on my sampler became a way to clear my head and occupy my hands. So, over the next couple of days, I brought my sampler with me everywhere. If I was bored, I would work on it. By the time I was almost done, I could sew almost twice as quickly as I could when I started. 
A couple tips, if you're ever in the same situation:
1. When you tie a knot on one end of your thread, trim the excess thread behind that. I would always get it stuck in my stitches when I started and as soon as I trimmed it, I eliminated at least half of my tangles. 
2. Often, the diagrams you see are meaningless. Sometimes, the videos just aren't that helpful either. Don't be discouraged. Imitate the videos as they play, and run them on half speed if they go too quickly. If that doesn't work, find another video. Seeing something from a different angle can make all the difference.
3. When you sew a stitch, find a repeatable, comfortable motion. When I started coral stitch, my right hand was cramping because I was holding the needle from the very end when I needed to hold it higher up for a moment to get the needle through the fabric and then right back up. Once I adjusted, it was a breeze.
4. If sewing alone is boring, find a podcast, show, or movie to play just for noise. Just the fact that you're hearing something can be enough to go from boredom to entertainment.
